# Pitch facts and judge Q&A

## Ten facts to use on stage (each is sourced)
1. A message to Mars takes 4 to 24 minutes each way, so a question and answer can take up to 48 minutes. [A: NASA]
2. NASA and Google are already testing an AI medical assistant (CMO-DA) for crews that cannot reach Earth. Our project is the monitoring layer for that idea. [B: TechCrunch, Space.com]
3. NASA's career radiation limit is 600 mSv. At the measured Mars cruise rate of 1.84 mSv per day, a crew reaches it in about 326 days. [A]
4. NASA schedules 8.5 hours of sleep but ISS crews average about 6.1. About 75 percent use sleep medicine. [A: Lancet Neurology 2014]
5. ISS crews reported CO2 symptoms from 2.3 mmHg, well below older limits. Limits had to be tightened. [A: NASA]
6. In a NASA model, with the air fans off, CO2 around a crew member hits a dangerous 10 mmHg in about 2 hours. [A]
7. Astronauts lose about 1 to 1.5 percent of bone density per month, and calf muscle volume falls about 13 percent in 6 months despite exercise. [A]
8. People respond very differently: some astronauts lost over 30 percent of hip strength in 6 months, others none. This is why we use PERSONAL baselines. [A]
9. NASA already uses a 3-minute reaction test (PVT-B) on the ISS to catch fatigue. Webcam PERCLOS correlates highly with it (r = 0.875). [A]
10. NASA lists a formal Behavioral Health Risk for deep missions, and says Moon and Mars countermeasures are not yet validated. [A]

## Likely judge questions
Q: Why not just call Mission Control?
A: On Mars the reply takes up to 48 minutes. In an emergency the crew must act first. Our distance mode and Autonomy Meter show this live.

Q: Is the AI replacing the doctor?
A: No. It supports the crew medical officer, answers only from sourced notes, never gives drug doses, and escalates dangerous cases. NASA's own CMO-DA is built on the same principle.

Q: Are your thresholds official NASA numbers?
A: The limits (600 mSv, 3 mmHg CO2, 355 ms lapse and so on) are NASA's. The green, yellow and red bands are our own choices, and we label them that way.

Q: Is your data real?
A: The limits and evidence are real and sourced. The astronaut data in the demo is simulated, because real astronaut health data is not public.

Q: How accurate is the webcam fatigue check?
A: It is a screening aid. Simple versions reach 80 to 92 percent in small tests and drop with glasses or poor light. We calibrate per person and confirm with the reaction test.

Q: What if the language model makes something up?
A: It can only use our notes, must name the file and evidence grade, and says "not in my notes" otherwise. It runs locally, so it works with no internet.

Q: What is new compared with existing astronaut monitoring?
A: Personal baselines, several weak signals combined, offline AI triage, and delay-aware sync, all in one on-board system built for Earth-independent missions.

Q: What would you do with more time?
A: Validate thresholds with a flight surgeon, test on real wearable data, and add NASA's validated mood scales.
