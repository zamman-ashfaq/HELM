# Mental stress and cognition

## What it measures
A short daily mood check-in, a cognitive test, sleep pattern, and optional webcam or
voice screening.

## Key facts
- NASA lists a formal Behavioral Health Risk: isolation and confinement can reduce
  cognitive and behavioural functioning. Selection, training and ISS countermeasures
  have mostly worked, but countermeasures for Moon and Mars have not been validated.
  [A: NASA Human Research Program risk page, updated Sep 2025]
- NASA's HFBP programme studies isolation, confinement, sleep loss, work overload,
  radiation and weightlessness together. [A: nasa.gov HFBP]
- APEX study (121 people in 40 teams in isolation): positive mood fell over time,
  while stress and negative mood varied a lot between people and followed
  situational stressors more than the length of isolation. [A: NASA NTRS 20260002661]
- Real in-flight tools used by NASA: weekly Beck Depression Inventory-II, Profile of
  Mood States (POMS), a conflict questionnaire, and the PVT-B reaction test.
  [A: Mollicone et al., NASA Reaction Self-Test papers]
- The Cognition battery has 10 brief tests (about 16 minutes on ISS). In ground
  simulations, sleep loss lowered vigilance and processing speed. [A: Basner et al.]
- Exercise is described by NASA researchers as a psychological countermeasure. [B/A]
- Mars adds delayed contact with family and ground support, since messages take
  4 to 24 minutes each way. [A: NASA]

## What to track in the app
Daily 0 to 10 stress score, mood trend over 14 days, sleep, PVT-B result, exercise
completed, and conflict or team-tension note. Show a TREND, not just today's value.

## Demo thresholds [TEAM]
Stress score: green 0 to 3, yellow 4 to 6, red 7 to 10. Falling mood trend over 14 days
is a yellow flag. Note: the 0 to 10 check-in is our simplified scale. NASA uses
validated instruments (POMS, BDI-II), which we do not reproduce.

## Actions
Offer a private check-in, protect rest and exercise, rotate tasks, arrange contact
with ground support (delayed on Mars), and escalate to the commander or crew medical
officer if the person shows hopelessness, self-harm thoughts or severe agitation.
