# Heart and fitness (heart, bone, muscle, exercise)

## What it measures
Heart rate and heart rate variability (HRV, wearable), blood pressure, daily exercise
log, and (on the ground or by scan) bone and muscle changes.

## Heart and blood vessels
- No NASA universal heart-rate limit exists. Compare each astronaut to their OWN
  baseline. This is the core idea of our personal-baseline approach. [TEAM design]
- Six astronauts on 52 to 199 day ISS missions showed smaller heart and circulation
  changes than expected. Authors credit daily exercise. Study summaries differ on the
  exact in-flight changes, so we only use the main conclusion.
  [A: Hughson et al., J Applied Physiology 2012]
- HRV (the variation between heartbeats) reflects nervous-system control of the heart.
  In 20 ISS astronauts, parasympathetic (rest-and-recover) activity was lower in
  flight than before flight. [A/B: Otsuka research group, preprint 2022]
- After landing, heart-rate control is still disturbed at 5 days and recovers almost
  fully by about 1 month. [A: PMC3628896]
- Other NASA-listed deconditioning risks: fluid shifts toward the head, balance
  problems, and Spaceflight Associated Neuro-ocular Syndrome (SANS, eye and vision
  changes), about 2 per mission in NASA's sample medical model output. [A]

## Exercise on the ISS [A]
- Crews get 2.5 hours per day for exercise including setup and cleanup, roughly 1.5 h
  resistance and 1 h aerobic. Roughly 6 days a week [B].
- Equipment: cycle ergometer, T2 treadmill, and the ARED resistance device.
- Exercise also helps mental well-being.

## Bone [A]
- Average bone mineral density loss is about 1 to 1.5 percent per month at weight-bearing
  sites (NASA NTRS). Hip about 1.5 percent per month, lumbar spine about 1 percent,
  whole body about 0.3 percent (NASA Sibonga slides).
- One month in space can equal the density loss a post-menopausal woman has in a year.
- Hip bone strength fell about 2.4 to 2.7 percent per month in ISS crews (computer
  modelling). Some individuals lost over 30 percent of hip strength in 6 months.
  Recovery is incomplete for some bone structure after a year.
- ARED exercise helps but does not fully stop hip loss. A bone drug plus ARED did
  better in a NASA study. [A: Lang et al., Sibonga et al.]

## Muscle [A]
- ISS crews after 6 months with exercise: calf muscle volume down 13 percent, calf peak
  power down 32 percent (Trappe et al. 2009). Thigh volume down about 4 to 7 percent,
  calf 10 to 16 percent, upper arm unchanged (Genc et al.).
- Older short-flight data (5 to 11 days, before modern equipment) showed up to 20
  percent muscle loss (Canadian Space Agency). Use the 13 percent figure for long missions.
- Individuals vary a lot: in a calf-muscle biopsy study one astronaut lost 51 percent of
  muscle fibre size, while another showed no loss in a main calf muscle. [A/B]

## Demo thresholds [TEAM]
Resting heart rate vs own baseline: green within 10 percent, yellow 10 to 20 percent
above, red above 20 percent. HRV: yellow if 1.5 standard deviations below own baseline,
red at 2.5. Exercise completion: yellow below 80 percent of the plan, red below 50.

## Actions
Complete the exercise plan, check for infection, dehydration or overtraining when
resting heart rate rises, report chest pain or fainting at once.
