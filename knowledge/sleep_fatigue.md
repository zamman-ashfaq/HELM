# Sleep and fatigue

## What it measures
Hours slept (wearable), wake-ups, sleep diary, and a 3-minute reaction test (PVT-B).

## Key facts from the largest astronaut sleep study [A: Barger et al., Lancet Neurology 2014]
- Data: 64 astronauts on 80 shuttle missions and 21 on ISS, over 4,000 nights on Earth
  and 4,200 nights in space.
- NASA schedules 8.5 hours of sleep. Actual average: 5.96 h (shuttle), 6.09 h (ISS).
- Only 12 percent (shuttle) and 24 percent (ISS) of sleeps reached 7 hours, versus
  42 and 50 percent after returning home.
- Sleep loss starts before flight: under 6.5 hours per night in the three months of training.
- Sleep medicine: about 75 percent of ISS crew used it at some point. Researchers warn
  that if all crew are medicated, response to an emergency alarm could be poor.
- Ground studies link under 6 hours of sleep to worse performance. The sun rises and
  sets every 90 minutes in orbit, which disrupts the body clock.

## The reaction test (PVT-B) [A: NASA Reaction Self-Test, Basner and Dinges]
- 3 minutes. A light appears at random 2 to 5 seconds apart and the person presses a
  button. NASA put it on the ISS in 2009 as the Reaction Self-Test.
- Lapse = reaction time of 355 ms or more (the 10-minute version uses 500 ms).
- False start = pressing with no stimulus or under 130 ms.
- Best single signal of sleep loss: response speed (1 divided by reaction time).
- It is part of NASA's Cognition battery. Astronauts needed about 16 minutes for the
  whole battery. [A: Basner et al. 2015, PMC4691281]

## Related heart-rate finding
In a study of 10 ISS astronauts, sleep duration fell in 7 and rose in 3 after
reaching space. Sleep can be estimated from heart-rate patterns. [A: Otsuka 2021]

## Demo thresholds [TEAM]
Sleep per night: green 7 h or more, yellow 6 to 7, red under 6. Use a 3-night rolling average.
PVT-B lapses and slower response speed: compare to the person's own baseline
(yellow z-score 1.5, red 2.5).

## Actions
Protect the sleep schedule, adjust cabin light, reduce night workload, limit sleep
medicine, avoid safety-critical tasks after bad nights, tell the commander.
