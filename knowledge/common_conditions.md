# Common and high-consequence conditions in spaceflight (for the advisor)

Source: NASA Integrated Medical Model (IMM), NASA-STD-3001 Appendix E, and in-flight
event records from Shuttle, Mir and ISS. [A]
These lists tell the advisor what is likely. They are not a diagnosis tool.

## Space adaptation conditions
Back pain, constipation, headache, insomnia, nasal congestion, nosebleed, space motion
sickness, urinary incontinence and urinary retention. They tend to occur early in a
flight and do not depend on mission length. [A: NASA NTRS 20100023129]

## Observed ISS and shuttle records
- Space adaptation syndrome made up about 42 percent of recorded in-flight medical
  events in one summary. [B]
- About 75 percent of shuttle astronauts took medicine at some point for motion
  sickness, headache, sleeplessness or back pain. [B]
- Most in-flight problems are not emergencies and can be treated on board. In a few
  cases astronauts returned early for kidney stones or irregular heartbeat. [B]

## Conditions on NASA's high-consequence list (ESCALATE)
Stroke, seizure, head injury, anaphylaxis, sepsis, choking or blocked airway, smoke
inhalation, toxic exposure (for example ammonia), chest injury, acute radiation
syndrome, decompression sickness during spacewalks, small bowel obstruction,
medication adverse reaction. [A: NASA IMM condition list]
